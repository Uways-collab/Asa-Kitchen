Our Story

Àṣà means culture.
We cook it daily.

In Yoruba, àṣà is the living thread of culture — the way a people remember, celebrate and gather. Our kitchen began with that idea: that the flavours of home deserve a place of honour.

Chef Tolu Adeyemi grew up between her grandmother's wood-fired kitchen in Abeokuta and the restless markets of Lagos. Today she marries those memories with modern fire cookery — every dish built on slow stocks, house-milled spice and produce from Nigerian farms and coasts.

The result is food that feels both new and deeply familiar. Come as a guest. Leave as family.

The Experience

More than dinner.
A whole evening.

Every detail at Àṣà is designed to slow the night down — the glow of the coals, the scent of suya spice, the rhythm of highlife drifting from the bar.

  • The Chef's Table

    Eight seats facing the open fire. A seven-course menu cooked in front of you — ₦85,000 per guest.

  • Private Dining

    Our candlelit private room for 8–14 guests, with bespoke menus and dedicated service.

  • Sunday Table

    A slow, shared family-style lunch every Sunday — platters, stories and live palm-wine highlife.

The Chef

Tolu Adeyemi

Chef & Founder

“I cook the way my grandmother taught me — with patience, with fire, and with respect for every ingredient that comes from our soil. Àṣà is simply that: culture, on a plate, for everyone at the table.”

After stages in Accra and London, Chef Tolu returned home to build a kitchen that treats Nigerian produce with the same reverence as any fine-dining pantry. Her food has been celebrated for making the familiar feel extraordinary.

— Tolu
